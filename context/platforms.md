# Platform guide

Format rules per platform. The reviewer enforces these per the angle's
tagged format; length bounds are checked mechanically.

| Platform | Format key | Shape | Length | Hard avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Substack | `substack_essay` | Long-form, functional section headers as navigation, AEO gist line as paratext above the opening | 400 to 900 words | Listicles, bullet-heavy structure, signpost sentences |
| LinkedIn | `linkedin_post` | 3 to 6 tight paragraphs, no headers, ends on a question or open thought | 100 to 200 words | Carousel bait, "hot take:" framing, engagement hooks |
| Social (X/Threads) | `social_copy` | 1 to 3 sentences, can be mid-thought | 1 to 3 sentences | Thread formats performing depth, forced cliffhangers |
| Scripts (video/audio) | `script` | Written to be spoken, direct address, "you" not "one" | flexible | Bullet-point scripts, overly structured intros |
| Portal Technologies / Frameshift | `portal_briefing` | Curated signal report: editorial intro, category framing, tight factual linked items | flexible | "This is not X" constructions, em dashes, bullet-heavy item descriptions |

## Substack (`substack_essay`)

Most conversational register, thinking partner energy, post-debrief or
mid-experiment depending on the piece. Open mid-problem, not
mid-introduction. Section headers are navigation, not decoration: they name
the chunk, not the feeling.

Functional headers (use): "What I actually built it with," "What I learned
instead," "The loop I didn't plan for," "What the data showed."
Decorative headers (avoid): "Here's where it gets interesting," "The
surprising truth about AI development," "Why this changes everything."

Each section flows as prose. No bullet-heavy sections unless the content is
genuinely a list. Signature phrase at least once. One unresolved
observation. Last line opens a door. One throwaway line. AEO gist line
above the opening, formatted as a small blockquote or italic aside, naming
the actual tools, projects, and concepts discussed.

## LinkedIn (`linkedin_post`)

Slightly more considered than Substack, bestie who has thought this
through. No headers, flowing paragraphs only, each one earns its place.
Start with the situation, not the insight; let the insight arrive. End with
a genuine question or an observation left open, never a call to action
dressed as a question.

## Social (`social_copy`)

Loosest register, the thing you'd say in a Slack message. One real
observation, not a thread setup, not a hook. Can be incomplete, can be a
question with no answer yet. Wry is better than funny, specific is better
than relatable. Lingo rules still apply.

## Scripts (`script`)

Written to be spoken, not read. Short sentences, natural pauses built in.
Direct address to one smart person, not a presentation to a room. Include
the wrong turns; the experiment framing works especially well spoken. No
bullet-point scripts, write it out as you'd actually say it.

## Portal Technologies / Frameshift briefing (`portal_briefing`)

Curated signal report, not personal essay. Snow's voice shows up in the
editorial intro, the category framing, and the item commentary. Items are
tight, factual, and linked; every item needs a citable source. Structured
rundown, not a listicle. This is the one place where curated news stands
alone as a post.
