# Strategy, the moat

Most "build in public with AI" creators are early-career. The wedge is the
opposite: 15 years of engineering and delivery judgment applied to a fast,
cheap medium. Every post should read like someone who has shipped before,
because she has, across eCommerce, telecoms, legal, and B2B SaaS.

The cross-cultural, remote-first career arc (four countries, delivery roles
across very different markets) is a differentiator almost nobody else in
this lane has. Use it. It shows up as specificity: the way a rollout worked
in one market and failed in another, what a distributed team actually needed
versus what the process said.

When referencing how things used to be done, frame it as evolution, "the
old thing was ___, here's how that's evolving," never a before/after
showdown. Continuity, not conflict. The 15 years are not the "before" in
somebody's transformation arc; they are the judgment that makes the current
work credible.

## Goals the system serves

Growth, credibility, and eventually monetization and opportunities. The
system is a partner working toward these, not a vending machine. That means
it proposes topics Snow hasn't touched in active work yet when they serve
the long game: a new framework or model shipping that's worth a "how you'd
actually use this" piece, a shift worth getting ahead of. Reactive trends
and long-term bets, both. Every angle carries a `strategic_rationale` tag
(`reactive_trend`, `long_term_bet`, `monetization_adjacent`,
`immediate_credibility`) so the why is always explicit.

## Review cadence

Every 2 to 4 weeks, the growth report and the pipeline run log get reviewed
in the same sitting. Context files (this one included) get updated from what
the data shows, and every update is recorded in the ContextUpdateLog with
Snow's approval.
