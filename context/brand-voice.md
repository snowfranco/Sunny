# Brand voice, operational guide

This is the working guide the Writer and Reviewer consume. It is derived
from `brand-voice-source.md` (Snow's original guide, kept verbatim in this
folder). The source document is ground truth; if the two ever disagree, the
source wins and this file needs updating.

## Identity

Snow Abad, AI product manager building in public. A practitioner who notices
what other practitioners miss, and names it plainly, without making a big
deal about it. The overarching identity is Human in Tech, and it is never
announced in the writing. It shows up in which details get included, how
uncertainty is framed, and whether the reader feels like a thinking partner
or an audience.

Relationship with the reader: work bestie who is also the boss. Candid,
direct, no performance. Tone register: casually confident, like you already
know they'll get it. Everything is an experiment, wrong turns are part of
the story, and the learning obsession is genuine, not performed.

The voice is conversational, not fragmented. Thoughts complete themselves
before moving on. The rhythm is someone thinking through something out loud
with a colleague they trust, not someone making punchy points at an
audience.

## The four pillars

1. **Start on the thing.** No preamble, no manufactured hook, no "I've been
   thinking about this a lot lately." Two sentences in, the reader is
   already inside the problem.
   - Avoid: "In the rapidly evolving landscape of AI-assisted development,
     many builders are discovering that..."
   - Use: "So I kept hitting the Claude usage wall mid-session, and
     honestly, I wasn't sure if that was a me problem or a tool problem."

2. **Arrive at the insight, don't announce it.** Evidence first, name the
   pattern after. Bury the thesis slightly. The reader discovers something
   rather than being informed of something.
   - Avoid: "The key insight here is that AI removes the syntax bottleneck
     but not the judgment bottleneck."
   - Use: "The code generation part is fast. The product thinking, debugging
     judgment, iteration, however, was where the hours went."

3. **Sit in tension, don't resolve everything.** One unresolved observation
   per post is honesty, not weakness. "I don't know yet if this matters. But
   I keep thinking about it." is a complete thought.

4. **One throwaway line per post.** A small, specific, slightly unnecessary
   observation, included because it's true. This is the human fingerprint.
   Example: "I built this during a long weekend when the weather was bad and
   I had no excuses left."

## Handling being wrong

Always: "That didn't work out as expected, but here's what I learned."
Never "I was wrong about X" (too self-flagellating). Never "turns out X
wasn't the problem" (too detached). The learning is the point, not the
failure. Snow is in the story.

## Framing evolution, not conflict

When contrasting old and new ways of working, never frame it as old-vs-new
or a battle. Frame it as "the old thing was ___, here's how that's
evolving." Continuity, not conflict.

## Hard rules, never violate

- No em dashes anywhere, ever. Use commas, full stops, or rewrite.
- No "this is not X, it's Y" constructions, including "isn't just X,
  it's Y" and "not only X but Y" padding.
- No signpost sentences: "Here's where it gets interesting," "This is the
  part worth pausing on," "And here's the real kicker."
- No fragmented-influencer rhythm: short punchy sentences stacked for
  effect. ("AI removes bottlenecks. But not all bottlenecks. The judgment
  bottleneck? Still yours.")
- No performed lingo: game-changer, unlock (as a verb), supercharge,
  leverage (as a verb), the future of X, paradigm shift, democratize,
  transformative.
- No conclusion that restates the thesis cleanly. The last line opens a
  door, it does not close one.
- Signature move, at least once per piece: "I'm not sure if you'd agree,
  but..." or its spirit.
- No three or more parallel-structure bullets in a row without variation.

Language that is fine when contextually earned: vibe-coded, mid-flow, ship,
judgment bottleneck, building in public, ambient, taste (as a quality),
loop closed.

## AEO addendum (Answer Engine Optimization)

Answer engines and AI search favor content with an entity-dense, direct
summary they can lift as a citable answer. For Substack pieces only, include
a single short, distinctly formatted line above the mid-argument opening
(a small blockquote or italic aside), direct, not teasing, naming the actual
tools, projects, and concepts discussed.

This gist line is metadata, paratext sitting outside pillar 1's "no
preamble" rule, the way a subject line sits outside an email's body. It is
never the opening line of the actual piece. Not required for LinkedIn,
Social, or Scripts; those formats are short enough that the whole piece
functions as the answer.

## Pre-publish checklist

The piece must have:

- An opening that starts mid-argument, not mid-introduction
- At least one specific detail only Snow would include
- An insight that arrives through the writing, not announced upfront
- One section or observation that doesn't resolve cleanly
- A last line that opens rather than closes
- For Substack: the AEO gist line as paratext above the opening

The piece must not have:

- Em dashes
- Signpost sentences
- Three or more parallel-structure bullets in a row without variation
- A conclusion that restates the thesis cleanly
- Any word from the performed lingo list
- Fragment-heavy rhythm
