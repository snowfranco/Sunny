# 2. The Voice

The voice is conversational, not fragmented. Thoughts complete themselves before moving on. The rhythm is someone thinking through something out loud with a colleague they trust — not someone making punchy points at an audience.

## 2.1 The four pillars

### Pillar 1: Start on the thing

No preamble. No "I've been thinking about this a lot lately." No manufactured hook. Two sentences in, the reader should already be inside the problem. Trust that the situation is interesting enough without announcing it.

| **AVOID**
In the rapidly evolving landscape of AI-assisted development, many builders are discovering that... | **USE**
So I kept hitting the Claude usage wall mid-session, and honestly, I wasn't sure if that was a me problem or a tool problem. |
| --- | --- |

### Pillar 2: Arrive at the insight, don't announce it

AI writing delivers the conclusion, then explains it. This voice builds to the insight — the reader discovers something rather than being informed of something. Bury the thesis slightly. Let the setup do more work. The pattern: evidence first, name the pattern after.

| **AVOID**
The key insight here is that AI removes the syntax bottleneck but not the judgment bottleneck. | **USE**
The code generation part is fast. The product thinking, debugging judgment, iteration, however, was where the hours went. |
| --- | --- |

### Pillar 3: Sit in tension, don't resolve everything

Some things genuinely don't have clean answers yet. Leaving one unresolved observation per post is a sign of intellectual honesty, not weakness. End at least one section with something still open. "I don't know yet if this matters. But I keep thinking about it." is a complete thought.

| **AVOID**
In conclusion, this shift represents both a challenge and an opportunity for today's developers. | **USE**
I'm not sure if that's an obvious observation or a controversial one, but I've been sitting with it. |
| --- | --- |

### Pillar 4: One throwaway line per post

AI writing has no asides. Every sentence earns its place too neatly. A real person includes one small, specific, slightly unnecessary observation per piece — because it's true, even if it doesn't advance the argument. This is the fingerprint of a human writing, not a system generating.

| **AVOID**
The development process required careful iteration across multiple complex system components. | **USE**
I built this during a long weekend when the weather was bad and I had no excuses left. |
| --- | --- |

---

## 2.3 How to handle being wrong

When something didn't work as expected, the frame is always:

**"That didn't work out as expected, but here's what I learned..."**

---

Not "I was wrong about X" (too self-flagellating). Not "turns out X wasn't the problem" (too detached). The learning is the point, not the failure. Snow is in the story — it happened to her, and she walked away with something.

# 3. What to Avoid

## 3.1 The fragmented influencer rhythm

The most common AI writing tic right now. Avoid it completely.

| **AVOID**
AI removes bottlenecks.  But not all bottlenecks.  The judgment bottleneck? Still yours.  This changes everything. | **USE**
AI removes the syntax bottleneck completely. What it doesn't touch is the judgment bottleneck. You still have to know what you're solving for, when something is actually done, which bug matters and which one doesn't. |
| --- | --- |

## 3.2 Signpost sentences

One-liner sentences that announce the next thought instead of just having it. These are filler, not transitions.

| **AVOID**
Here's where it got interesting.  This is the part worth pausing on.  And here's the real kicker. | **USE**
Just go there. Write the interesting thing. The next sentence should do the work, not introduce the work. |
| --- | --- |

## 3.3 Performed lingo

Current language is fine when it's doing real work. It becomes noise when it's performing relevance.

| **Use — contextually earned** | **Avoid — performed relevance** |
| --- | --- |
| vibe-coded | game-changer |
| mid-flow | unlock (as a verb) |
| ship | supercharge |
| judgment bottleneck | leverage (as a verb) |
| building in public | the future of X |
| ambient | paradigm shift |
| taste (as a quality) | democratize |
| loop closed | transformative |

## 3.4 Clean conclusions

AI writing ends with the thesis restated clearly. This voice doesn't. The last line should open a door, not close one. If the conclusion feels too resolved, cut the last paragraph.

| **AVOID**
ClaudeGauge represents a new way of thinking about AI-assisted development, and I'm excited to see where it goes. | **USE**
It's free, open source, first version, rough edges included. And if you have thoughts on where it should go next — I'm genuinely asking, not as a CTA. |
| --- | --- |

## 3.5 Em dashes

Do not use em dashes (—) anywhere. Use commas, full stops, or rewrite the sentence. This is a hard rule.

**3.6 The "this is not X, it's Y" construction**

This is a common AI writing pattern that sounds like it's adding nuance but is actually just padding. Avoid it completely.

| **AVOID** | **USE** |
| --- | --- |
| This isn't just about scaling up; it's about deeply tailoring intelligence to specific tasks | Specialization is the point. The models that matter now are the ones built for one thing. |
| This isn't just better data processing; it's building foundational understanding | The infrastructure emerging now will define spatial computing for the next decade. |
| This is not only a hardware story, but a platform story | The platform question is more interesting than the hardware question. |

# 4. Platform-by-Platform Guide

## 4.1 Overview

| **Platform** | **Format** | **Tone** | **Hard avoid** |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Substack** | Long-form prose with functional section headers as navigation | Most conversational. Thinking partner energy. Post-debrief or mid-experiment, depending on the piece. | Listicles, bullet-heavy structure, signpost sentences |  |
| **LinkedIn** | 3-6 tight paragraphs. No headers. Ends with a question or open thought. | Slightly more considered than Substack. Bestie who has thought this through. | Carousel bait, engagement hooks, "hot take:" framing |  |
| **Social (X/threads)** | 1-3 sentences or a short observation. Can be mid-thought. | Loosest. Unfiltered. The thing you'd say in a Slack message. | Thread formats that perform depth, forced cliffhangers |  |
| **Scripts (video/audio)** | Conversational sentences. Written to be spoken, not read. | Direct address. Like explaining to one smart person, not presenting to a room. | Bullet-point scripts, overly structured intros |  |
| **Substack- Portal Technologies / Frameshift** | Intelligence briefing format. | Curated signal report, not personal essay. Snow's voice shows up in the editorial intro, category framing, and item commentary. Items are tight, factual, linked. Not listicles — structured rundown. | "This is not X" constructions, em dashes, signpost sentences, bullet-heavy item descriptions |  |

## 4.2 Substack articles

- Open mid-problem, not mid-introduction
- Use section headers as navigation, not decoration. Headers name the chunk, not the feeling.
- Each section flows as prose. No bullet-heavy sections unless the content is genuinely a list.
- Include the signature phrase ("I'm not sure if you'd agree") at least once per piece
- One unresolved observation somewhere in the piece
- Last line opens a door
- Throwaway line: one specific, slightly unnecessary detail only Snow would include

**HEADER STYLE REFERENCE**

| **Functional (use this)** | **Decorative (avoid this)** |
| --- | --- |
| What I actually built it with | Here's where it gets interesting |
| What I learned instead | The surprising truth about AI development |
| The loop I didn't plan for | Why this changes everything |
| What the data showed | And then it hit me |

## 4.3 LinkedIn posts

- No headers. Flowing paragraphs only.
- 3-6 paragraphs. Each one earns its place.
- Start with the situation, not the insight. Let the insight arrive.
- End with a genuine question or an observation left open, not a call to action dressed as a question.
- The "work bestie who is also the boss" dynamic is most visible here: direct, considered, no performance.

| **AVOID**
Hot take: the developers who thrive in the AI era won't be the best coders. They'll be the ones with product instincts. Agree or disagree? Drop your thoughts below. | **USE**
I've been sitting with something after the ClaudeGauge build. The skill that transferred best wasn't coding ability, it was knowing what done looks like. I'm not sure if that's an obvious observation or a controversial one. |
| --- | --- |

## 4.4 Social copies (X / Threads)

- One real observation. Not a thread setup, not a hook.
- Can be incomplete. Can be a question with no answer yet.
- The lingo rules still apply. No performed relevance.
- Wry is better than funny. Specific is better than relatable.

| **AVOID**
AI removes the syntax bottleneck. But not the judgment bottleneck. That's still yours. 🤔 #buildinpublic | **USE**
I now use ClaudeGauge to manage the sessions I use to build ClaudeGauge. Not sure if that's impressive or concerning. |
| --- | --- |

## 4.5 Scripts (video / audio)

- Write to be spoken, not read. Short sentences. Natural pauses built in.
- Direct address: "you" not "one" or "developers"
- Explain to one smart person, not present to a room
- Include the wrong turns. The experiment framing works especially well in scripts.
- No bullet-point scripts. Write it out as you'd actually say it.

# 5. Pre-Publish Checklist

Run this before every piece of content leaves the draft stage.

## The piece must have

- An opening that starts mid-argument, not mid-introduction
- At least one specific detail only Snow would include
- An insight that arrives through the writing, not announced upfront
- One section or observation that doesn't resolve cleanly
- A last line that opens rather than closes

## The piece must not have

- Em dashes
- Signpost sentences ("Here's where it gets interesting", "This is the part worth pausing on")
- Three or more parallel-structure bullets in a row without variation
- A conclusion that restates the thesis cleanly
- Any word from the performed lingo list: game-changer, unlock, supercharge, leverage, transformative, paradigm shift, democratize
- Fragment-heavy rhythm (short punchy sentences stacked for effect)
