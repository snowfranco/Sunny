People keep asking me the same question in interviews, so let me answer it here. The question is why I would switch into AI product work fifteen years into a delivery career. And my honest answer is that I am not switching. The job followed me.

Here's what I mean. I have shipped software in eCommerce, in telecoms, in legal, in B2B SaaS, four industries, four countries, and the work underneath was always the same three questions. What are we actually solving? How will we know it's done? And what happens when it fails? The stack around those questions kept changing. The questions never did.

So when I started building my own AI pipeline this year, you can guess the first thing I wrote. It was not a prompt. It was not a model call. It was the escalation path, the exact thing that happens when the automated part fails and a human needs to decide. I wrote that before any AI code existed. A friend reviewing the repo laughed at me for it, and fair enough.

But that instinct is the fifteen years talking. A junior builder writes the happy path first, because the happy path is the demo, and the demo is the dopamine. You learn to write the failure path first by being the person on call when there wasn't one. I earned that lesson in a data center in a country I no longer live in, at around three in the morning, and I have never unlearned it.

That didn't work out as expected, by the way, my first attempt at explaining this in an interview. I rambled. Here's what I learned from the ramble: the transferable thing has a name, and the name is judgment, not experience. Experience is the receipts. Judgment is what the receipts bought.

I'm not sure if you'd agree with where I land on this, but I think the people best positioned for AI product work right now are the ones who have already shipped without it, because they know what done looks like when nobody is grading on effort.

What I still can't tell you is which half of my old playbook to throw away. Some of it is timeless and some of it is nostalgia, and they look identical from the inside. I'll report back when I can tell them apart.
