Fifteen years of delivery work across four countries, and the habit that survived every market, role, and tech stack change is embarrassingly small: a daily working notes file. Plain text, what happened, what I decided, what confused me.

I am back in school now, at Centennial College in Toronto, building freelance income and an AI portfolio between semesters. Most of what I knew fifteen years ago has been deprecated at least once. The notes habit is the thing still earning.

This month it started earning differently. The notes file now feeds a content pipeline I built, so the raw material of every post I write is whatever I actually did and decided that week. The habit did not change. What reads it did.

That didn't work out as expected when I first tried writing publicly years ago, but here's what I learned: I kept trying to write from expertise, and the notes kept insisting I write from the work itself. The second one is sustainable.

I'm not sure if you'd agree, but I would trade most credentials for a habit that compounds across careers. Which of yours has survived the longest?
